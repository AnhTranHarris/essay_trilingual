# Writing Style Protocol

## Status

**FOUNDATION ONLY. This file defines writing behavior; it contains no essay draft.**

## Citation and document standard

The final essay package will use **MLA 9th edition conventions** unless the USAJOBS application supplies a conflicting format requirement.

Default MLA presentation:
- 1-inch margins.
- Readable 12-point typeface.
- Double-spaced body text, quotations, and Works Cited.
- Left-aligned prose; no full justification.
- First-line paragraph indentation of 0.5 inch.
- MLA-style parenthetical in-text citations.
- A Works Cited section that matches the sources actually used.
- Block quotations and project-translated quotations handled according to MLA guidance.
- Page citations must come from the exact English edition actually cited.

Official MLA guidance is recorded in the project source registry and should be consulted during final formatting.

## Target writing level

The English essay should read at approximately:

**AP High School English analytical clarity**
+
**college-freshman creative-writing maturity where complexity requires it**

This means:
- clear thesis and topic sentences;
- concrete literary evidence before abstraction;
- precise vocabulary without unnecessary academic jargon;
- varied sentence length and rhythm;
- occasional creative or vivid phrasing when it clarifies an idea;
- explicit transitions between complicated concepts;
- explanations that a strong general reader can follow without specialist training;
- literary sophistication without graduate-seminar density.

The reader should not have to decode the prose to understand the argument.

## Human readability requirement

A Treasury hiring manager should be able to read the paper comfortably from beginning to end.

Prefer:
- direct sentences;
- natural paragraph lengths;
- clear argumentative progression;
- quotations integrated into the prose;
- specific nouns and verbs;
- restrained rhetorical flourish;
- explanations of translation concepts in ordinary language.

Avoid:
- inflated vocabulary used only to sound academic;
- repetitive thesis restatement;
- formulaic five-paragraph-essay transitions;
- excessive headings;
- mechanical "first, second, third" sequencing;
- unnecessary em dashes;
- uniform paragraph length;
- lists inside the literary essay unless academically justified;
- vague phrases such as "this shows" without explaining what and why.

## AI fingerprint policy

The paper should make sophisticated AI use **legible through method and execution**, not through robotic prose or artificial gimmicks.

Desirable AI-visible fingerprints:
- rigorously verified Fitzgerald quotations and citations;
- a controlled evidence ledger behind each literary claim;
- stable paragraph IDs across all three language versions;
- terminology consistency across translations;
- explicit control of semantic drift, ambiguity, register, imagery, and connotation;
- repeatable paragraph-to-paragraph translation alignment;
- independent back-translation or semantic QA;
- GitHub version history showing evidence checks, corrections, and translation verification;
- evidence that complex material was decomposed, checked, and recombined coherently.

Undesirable AI fingerprints:
- generic chatbot phrasing;
- overuse of words such as "delve," "tapestry," "multifaceted," or similar stock abstractions;
- excessive symmetry in sentence and paragraph structure;
- fake quotations or unverifiable citations;
- unexplained technical jargon;
- repetitive conclusions;
- writing that sounds more advanced than the underlying analysis;
- conspicuous attempts to advertise AI use inside the literary argument.

The desired impression is that the applicant knows how to use AI as a disciplined research, translation, and verification system while preserving pleasant, recognizably human-readable prose.

## Analytical voice

Use a confident but measured literary-analysis voice.

The writer may make interpretive claims, but should distinguish:
- what Fitzgerald's English text directly says;
- what can reasonably be inferred;
- where an interpretation remains debatable.

Avoid pretending that one interpretation is the only possible reading when the text supports several.

## Creative-writing bridge

College-freshman creative-writing techniques may be used selectively to make difficult analysis easier to follow.

Permitted techniques include:
- concrete sensory description when discussing imagery;
- controlled metaphor when explaining another metaphor;
- sentence rhythm that supports emphasis;
- brief narrative framing around an analytical transition;
- elegant callbacks to an earlier image or claim.

These techniques must serve analysis. They must not turn the paper into fiction or obscure the thesis.

## Trilingual consistency

The English essay is the canonical analytical document.

Spanish and Mandarin/Simplified-Chinese versions must preserve:
- the thesis;
- paragraph-level claims;
- evidence relationships;
- citation logic;
- degree of certainty;
- rhetorical intent.

The translations may adjust syntax, idiom, and sentence rhythm to read naturally in the target language. They should not imitate English grammar mechanically.

## GitHub QA visibility

GitHub is part of the disclosed production method.

The repository should make it possible to trace:
- evidence -> English paragraph;
- English paragraph -> Spanish paragraph;
- English paragraph -> Mandarin paragraph;
- translation QA finding -> correction;
- final paragraph -> verified state.

This provenance is intended to make quality control visible without cluttering the literary essay itself.

## Drafting gate

No essay drafting begins until:
1. source architecture is accepted;
2. MLA/style protocol is accepted;
3. authorship/provenance protocol is accepted;
4. the initial metaphor evidence set is verified.
