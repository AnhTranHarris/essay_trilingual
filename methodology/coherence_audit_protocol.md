# Coherence Audit Protocol

## Status

**FOUNDATION ONLY. This file defines the reasoning audit; it contains no essay draft.**

## Purpose

The essay must be coherent not only sentence-to-sentence and paragraph-to-paragraph, but also across the entire argument.

The project distinguishes two reasoning dimensions:

### Horizontal coherence

Horizontal review asks whether each local unit works on its own and connects correctly to the next unit.

Check:
- sentence logic;
- paragraph unity;
- topic sentence -> evidence -> interpretation -> transition;
- citation relevance;
- pronoun/reference clarity;
- local chronology;
- terminology consistency;
- whether a paragraph actually proves the claim it makes.

### Vertical coherence

Vertical review asks whether the complete paper remains logically consistent from introduction through conclusion.

Check:
- thesis stability;
- whether later paragraphs silently redefine earlier claims;
- whether the same metaphor receives incompatible interpretations without explanation;
- whether evidence used in one section undermines a claim made elsewhere;
- whether claims about Gatsby, Nick, Daisy, wealth, time, memory, class, desire, or the American Dream remain internally consistent;
- whether degree of certainty changes without justification;
- whether the conclusion accurately reflects the argument actually made;
- whether the essay develops rather than merely repeats its thesis.

## Contradiction rule

Contradiction is not automatically an error.

A contradiction may remain only when the paper explicitly identifies and explains it.

Acceptable examples:
- a metaphor may carry two meanings at different points in the novel;
- Nick may describe an image inconsistently because his understanding changes;
- Gatsby may embody both aspiration and self-deception;
- the essay may present a counter-reading before resolving or qualifying it.

When an apparent contradiction is intentional, the paper must tell the reader:
1. what the two positions are;
2. why they appear to conflict;
3. whether the conflict comes from character perspective, chronology, ambiguity, irony, changing context, or competing interpretations;
4. what conclusion the paper draws from that tension.

Unexplained contradiction is a QA failure.

## Argument ledger

Before final drafting, every major analytical claim should receive a stable claim ID:

- CLM-001
- CLM-002
- CLM-003
- ...

Each claim record should include:
- claim text;
- supporting evidence IDs;
- first paragraph using the claim;
- later paragraphs that depend on it;
- qualifications or exceptions;
- potential conflicts with other claims;
- resolution status.

This allows the essay to be inspected vertically rather than only as a sequence of paragraphs.

## Vertical audit passes

### Pass 1 — Thesis trace

Read only:
- introduction;
- first and last sentence of each body paragraph;
- conclusion.

Question:
Does the argument form one intelligible line from thesis to conclusion?

### Pass 2 — Claim dependency

For each major claim:
- identify where it is introduced;
- identify where it is developed;
- identify where it is qualified;
- identify where it is concluded.

Flag orphan claims, unsupported claims, and claims that disappear.

### Pass 3 — Contradiction scan

Compare all major claims pairwise where they address the same concept.

Flag:
- incompatible character interpretations;
- conflicting definitions;
- inconsistent treatment of a metaphor;
- chronology problems;
- changes in certainty;
- inconsistent causal claims.

Each flag must be either corrected or explicitly explained in the essay.

### Pass 4 — Evidence consistency

Check whether the same quotation or passage is being used to support mutually incompatible propositions.

If so, determine whether:
- the passage is genuinely ambiguous and the essay explains that ambiguity; or
- one use is weak and should be removed.

### Pass 5 — Concept continuity

Track recurring concepts through the whole essay.

Examples:
- green light;
- time;
- memory;
- wealth;
- class;
- illusion;
- identity;
- American Dream;
- distance;
- desire.

The meaning of a recurring term may evolve, but the evolution must be visible to the reader.

### Pass 6 — Conclusion integrity

The conclusion may synthesize, qualify, or extend the argument.

It must not introduce a major claim that the body never established.

## Translation coherence

Vertical coherence must survive translation.

After Spanish and Mandarin/Simplified-Chinese versions are produced:
- compare each translated claim ID to the English claim;
- verify that qualifications and contradictions remain visible;
- verify that a translated phrase has not accidentally turned a tentative claim into a definite one;
- verify that recurring concepts remain consistent across the whole translated document.

A translation can be locally accurate yet vertically wrong. Both dimensions must pass.

## Final coherence gate

The English essay cannot be frozen for translation until:
- horizontal paragraph review passes;
- vertical thesis trace passes;
- contradiction scan passes;
- claim dependency review passes;
- evidence consistency passes;
- conclusion integrity passes.

The Spanish and Mandarin versions cannot be finalized until the same vertical structure is preserved.

## Core rule

**Do not judge the paper only by whether each paragraph sounds good. Judge whether all paragraphs remain true to the same evolving argument when read as one system.**
