# Mandarin / Beijing Standard Chinese Translation Style Guide

## Status

**LOCKED TRANSLATION STANDARD**

The Mandarin version will use **Simplified Chinese in educated, contemporary Standard Mandarin written style associated with Beijing / Mainland China**, with broad national readability.

The goal is not to imitate Beijing street speech. A federal literary-analysis paper should sound like it was written by an educated Beijing reader in polished written Chinese, not like casual local conversation.

## Linguistic basis

The project follows modern Standard Chinese / Putonghua conventions:
- Beijing pronunciation is the phonological standard;
- Northern Mandarin is the dialectal base;
- exemplary modern vernacular Chinese is the grammatical norm.

For written presentation, use the currently effective Chinese punctuation standard **GB/T 15834-2011**.

Reference:
- Ministry of Education / Putonghua standard: https://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_xwfb/xw_fbh/moe_2128/moe_2326/moe_1144/tnull_14344.html
- GB/T 15834-2011 punctuation standard: https://openstd.samr.gov.cn/bzgk/std/newGbInfo?hcno=22EA6D162E4110E752259661E1A0D0A8

## Target voice

Use:

**educated Beijing / Mainland written Mandarin + national readability + literary-analytical clarity**

The Chinese should sound natural to a university-educated reader in Beijing while remaining normal and readable elsewhere in Mainland China.

Avoid:
- Beijing dialect spellings or heavy 儿化;
- local slang such as `倍儿`, `甭`, `得嘞`, or conversational Beijing-style particles;
- internet slang;
- Taiwan/Hong Kong-specific written vocabulary when a standard Mainland form is available;
- classical-Chinese compression that makes the essay sound archaic;
- literal English syntax;
- long chains of `的`;
- excessive passive `被` constructions;
- overuse of abstract nominalizations such as `进行……的实现`;
- AI-like repeated transitions such as `综上所述`, `值得注意的是`, `从某种意义上说` unless genuinely needed.

Prefer:
- concise modern written Chinese;
- shorter sentence units when the English sentence is too long for natural Chinese;
- explicit logical relationships;
- natural subject omission when context is clear;
- verbs instead of stacked abstract nouns;
- controlled literary elegance without academic grandiosity.

## Script and punctuation

Use:
- Simplified Chinese;
- full-width Chinese punctuation;
- Chinese quotation marks “ ” and ‘ ’;
- Chinese book-title marks 《 》 for work titles in Chinese prose;
- middle dot `·` for foreign personal names where appropriate;
- Chinese em dash `——` sparingly;
- Chinese ellipsis `……` if needed.

Follow GB/T 15834-2011 for punctuation behavior.

## Title and proper names

Use conventional Mainland forms where they are well established:

- *The Great Gatsby* -> 《了不起的盖茨比》
- F. Scott Fitzgerald -> F·斯科特·菲茨杰拉德
- Jay Gatsby -> 杰伊·盖茨比 / 盖茨比
- Nick Carraway -> 尼克·卡拉韦 / 尼克
- Daisy Buchanan -> 黛西·布坎南 / 黛西
- Tom Buchanan -> 汤姆·布坎南 / 汤姆
- James Gatz -> 詹姆斯·盖兹（James Gatz） on first use if needed

The Chinese title and name forms are linguistic conventions only. They do **not** make a published Chinese edition of the novel a source.

## Canonical relationship to English

The frozen English essay remains authoritative.

Chinese may change:
- syntax;
- sentence division;
- clause order;
- idiom;
- punctuation;
- rhetorical rhythm.

Chinese may not change:
- thesis;
- literary evidence;
- metaphor classification;
- degree of certainty;
- causal relationship;
- psychological-source limitations;
- intentional ambiguity;
- contradiction/tension structure;
- conclusion.

## Fitzgerald quotations

The 1925 English edition remains the literary authority.

For every Fitzgerald quotation:
1. map it to the verified English evidence ID;
2. translate directly from the verified English quotation;
3. preserve the image and metaphorical relationship;
4. do not imply that the wording comes from a published Chinese translation;
5. preserve the original English-edition page number;
6. identify the Chinese rendering as the authors' translation where needed.

Recommended disclosure on the title/process page:

`除特别说明外，文中菲茨杰拉德与弗洛伊德的中文引文均由本文作者依据所列英文版本译出；页码仍指向所引用的英文版本。`

## Literary-analysis register

Target approximately:

**strong upper-secondary / AP-equivalent analytical clarity + first-year university literary maturity**

The Chinese should not read like:
- a translation exercise;
- a Chinese graduate dissertation;
- a government policy document;
- a literary imitation of Fitzgerald;
- casual online commentary.

It should read like a polished analytical essay written for an educated general reader.

## Beijing-native-flow QA

Every paragraph must pass these checks.

### 1. Monolingual read
Read the Chinese without looking at the English.

Ask:
- Does this sound like written Chinese rather than translated English?
- Are subjects repeated unnecessarily?
- Are sentences too long?
- Are connectives excessive?
- Are abstract nouns replacing natural verbs?

### 2. Beijing/Mainland register check
Ask:
- Would an educated Beijing reader consider this standard, natural written Mandarin?
- Is any wording distinctly Taiwan/Hong Kong, archaic, slangy, or regionally marked?
- Is the diction formal without sounding bureaucratic?

### 3. Semantic-equivalence check
Compare with the frozen English paragraph.

Verify:
- same claim;
- same evidence;
- same qualification;
- same certainty;
- same metaphor relation;
- same intentional contradiction;
- same rhetorical purpose.

### 4. Literary-device check
Verify that Chinese wording does not accidentally turn:
- metaphor into simile;
- symbol into metaphor;
- personification into literal description;
- ambiguity into certainty.

### 5. Back-translation check
Produce an independent meaning-focused English back-translation.

It need not reproduce wording.

It must preserve:
- argument;
- causality;
- uncertainty;
- metaphor structure;
- source attribution;
- intentional tension.

Material drift requires revision.

## Vertical Chinese audit

After paragraph-level QA, review the Chinese essay as an independent complete paper.

Track:
- thesis stability;
- seven-metaphor progression;
- recurring terms;
- Gatsby's self-invention/restoration tension;
- Nick's tolerance/judgment tension;
- psychology-source boundaries;
- conclusion alignment.

A Chinese paragraph can be locally accurate yet still fail if the complete essay feels fragmented or changes its conceptual vocabulary across sections.

## Final gate

The Mandarin/Simplified-Chinese version cannot be frozen until it passes:

**Beijing/Mainland native-flow QA + semantic back-translation + citation QA + literary-device QA + horizontal coherence + vertical coherence + full visual render QA.**
