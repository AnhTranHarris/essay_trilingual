# Translation and Citation Protocol

## Scope

This file defines the process only. It contains no essay prose.

## Canonical text

The 1925 Charles Scribner's Sons first edition is the canonical text for literary quotations and page references.

Use the searchable Project Gutenberg transcription to locate candidate passages. Then verify every quoted phrase against the 1925 first-edition page image before it is accepted into the evidence ledger.

## Translation architecture

The English essay will be the canonical analytical version.

The Spanish and Mandarin versions will be faithful translations of that same analytical argument. They are not separate essays and must not introduce new substantive claims that are absent from the English version.

Each later paragraph should receive a stable paragraph identifier:

- EN-P001, EN-P002, ...
- ES-P001, ES-P002, ...
- ZH-P001, ZH-P002, ...

ES-P001 and ZH-P001 must correspond semantically to EN-P001.

## Fitzgerald quotations

Every quoted Fitzgerald passage must have:
- evidence ID
- chapter
- verified 1925 page number
- exact English transcription
- verification status
- later Spanish translation status
- later Chinese translation status

If we translate the quotation ourselves, record it as our translation. Do not present a machine- or project-generated translation as if it came from a published translator.

## Anti-hallucination rule

A literary quotation, page number, bibliographic claim, or translation-source claim cannot enter the final document unless its evidence record is VERIFIED.

Interpretive claims must distinguish:
- direct textual evidence
- reasonable literary inference
- outside critical scholarship

## Zero-cost rule

Do not require purchase of a book, article, database, or translation.

Commercial editions may be recorded bibliographically, but they are not source texts unless the user obtains legitimate no-cost access.
